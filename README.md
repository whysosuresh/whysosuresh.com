# whysosuresh.com

The source of [whysosuresh.com](https://whysosuresh.com), Suresh's home page: who he is, his latest videos, the things he makes, where else to find him, and a short page on how to [talk to him](https://whysosuresh.com/feedback).

## How it is built

- `site/` is the whole site: plain HTML, one stylesheet and the note flow's three scripts (`talk.js`, the screens; `talk-topics.js`, the topics a note can be about and their questions; `talk-send.js`, the bot check and the upload), with no build step. `site/_headers` sets the security headers on every file.
- `site/threads/` and `site/app/` are Suresh's private threads at `whysosuresh.com/threads`, where his inbox lives (`/inbox` redirects there): Today (new notes, and open threads that sat a week), the list (four tabs by where a thread stands, and the spaces), and each thread at its own address (`/threads/42-who-owns-what`) with its living summary on top, its text, then its comments and history in order. It keeps the inbox's one-key moves with Undo and its Reply, and adds comments, editing a thread's title, summary and text with a preview, new threads and ⌘K search, in Compact type, with a bar along the bottom on a phone. The page holds no data; it reads the threads' calls under `/threads/api/`, which Access guards. A thread's main section is its text, a **page** (a Claude artifact's own HTML, saved into the thread by the sync with `inbox page`, every version kept, shown in a sealed frame under a bar with its versions, Open in Claude and full screen), or a **link** (a card that opens an artifact that only works on claude.ai). Signed out, `/threads/` offers Sign in, and a thread's own address shows what a missing address shows, so it never says a thread exists. A thread can be shared: Suresh invites a person by their email address and name from the thread's Share (S), his Gmail opens a draft with the thread's `?invite` link for him to send, and they sign in with a code Cloudflare emails them and see exactly the threads they are invited to (their own home, with no tabs or moves), and comment on them; a comment marked "Only me" is never shown to them. Shared threads carry a people mark in every list, and Today shows the ones whose last word is theirs ("Waiting on you"). One of his own threads can also be **public**: anyone reads it at its address with no sign-in, a link to it previews with its title, and a visitor can send him a note on it, which shows on the thread only once he OKs it. He turns it on with the one switch in Share; a note waiting is a solid-bordered card on his page with Show it, Drop it and Reply by email (a Gmail draft), Today lists the threads with notes waiting, a globe marks a public thread in every list, and a comment on a public thread is written for anyone unless he picks the people here or Only me. **People** (under All threads, and on a phone's bar) lists everyone he deals with, whoever was last in touch first, with those only in threads he dropped folded away; each person's page (`/threads/people/7-meera`) shows every thread they are in and how, what they said, newest first, and his own notes on them, which only he sees; he can rename them and give a person with no address one, once. Every name on a thread opens that person, and ⌘K finds people too. Invited people never see any of it.
- `src/` is the Worker's code, which runs only for `/api/*` and `/threads/*` (the threads' calls, and the threads page at its addresses, handed over from `site/` through the assets binding). Everything Suresh keeps is a **thread** (`src/store.ts`): one row in D1's `threads` with a number people can say out loud (#42), its history in `events` (created, moved, its space changed, its text edited, with the text it replaced) and the words added to it in `comments`. `POST /api/talk` takes a typed note of up to 4,000 characters about one of the topics, as JSON, checks it with Cloudflare's Turnstile and two daily limits (20 a day from one network, 60 in all), and keeps it as a new thread, answering only its id. The private door checks Access's signature on every request itself (`src/access.ts`), against its Access application's tag, and admits only the owner, the sync, and people with an invitation that stands. Access asks the site at sign-in whether an address is invited (`POST /api/access/evaluate`, answered with a JWT signed by the site's evaluation key, whose public half is at `/api/access/keys`; `src/evaluate.ts`), and the door asks the same question of every call. An invited person's calls see only their threads (`src/store.ts`'s reader), and the door shapes what they see (`src/threads.ts`). The threads' calls (`src/threads.ts`) name a thread by its number: `GET` and `POST /threads/api/threads` list a tab and add, `GET /threads/api/threads/<n>` answers a thread with its comments and history, `POST /threads/api/threads/<n>` moves it, and `POST /threads/api/threads/<n>/comments` and `/text` add a comment and replace its title, its living summary or its main text (a visitor's own words are never rewritten); the owner's page starts threads of his own with the same `POST /threads/api/threads`, `GET /threads/api/search?q=` finds a thread by its number or words for ⌘K, and `GET /threads/api/sign-in?next=` is where a signed-out page sends him, Access signing him in on the way. `POST /threads/api/threads/<n>/pages` (the sync's) keeps an HTML document of up to 16 MiB as the thread's page's next version, in the private R2 bucket `whysosuresh-com-pages`; `GET /threads/api/threads/<n>/pages/<version>` serves it to the thread's frame, sealed by its own headers (`src/page.ts`); `POST /threads/api/threads/<n>/link` sets or clears the thread's link to a claude.ai address. `GET` and `POST /threads/api/threads/<n>/people` read, invite and remove the people a thread is shared with (the owner's alone; `people` and `members` in D1), a comment's `audience` `owner` keeps it from them, and `GET /threads/api/me` says who is calling. Everyone Suresh deals with is a **person** with a number (`src/people.ts`, `people` in D1): the people he invites, a visitor who wrote from the site or on a public thread with an address (one person per address, whatever its case), and someone who told him something in person (the sync names them by number, or a told name joins the one person who has it). `GET /threads/api/people` lists everyone, whoever was last in touch first, `GET /threads/api/people/<n>` answers one person with every thread they are in and how, and everything they said, and `POST /threads/api/people/<n>` (his alone) renames them, gives them an address once, or keeps his own notes on them; the owner's thread, comment and search answers name people by number. Invited people never read any of it. Each row of the owner's list also says how many people the thread is shared with (`shared`), whether an invited person had the last word (`waiting`), and how many visitors' notes wait for his OK (`pending`). The `publish` and `unpublish` moves (his alone, his own threads only) make a thread public; a comment's `audience` `public` is for anyone, and `POST /threads/api/threads/<n>/comments/<id>` with `show`, `drop` or `hold` is his OK on a visitor's note. A public thread is read with no sign-in (`src/public.ts`): `GET /api/threads/<n>` answers what it says and its public comments, `GET /api/threads/<n>/pages/<version>` its page, sealed as the private one is, and `POST /api/threads/<n>/notes` takes a visitor's note through the note flow's checks and limits; at its address the threads page carries its title and link-preview tags (`src/pages.ts`). Every write stamps the thread's last activity. `site/spaces.js` lists what a thread can be about: every topic, and the company. `migrations/` holds the database's tables.
- `tools/inbox/` is the inbox's command line, which the recorder sync and Suresh's other sessions run with Node as is (`node tools/inbox/inbox.ts --help`), on the threads' calls and by a thread's number: `list`, `add`, `move`, `comment` (Markdown on stdin; `--only-me` keeps it from the people a thread is shared with), `edit` (a thread's living summary or main text, on stdin), `show` (a thread with its comments, history, page versions, link and people), `page` (an HTML file as the thread's page), `link` (a claude.ai address, or `--clear`), `people` (everyone he deals with) and `person` (one person, by their number, with their threads and words), as the sync's service token, whose secret it reads from `INBOX_SYNC_CLIENT_SECRET` and never prints.
- `wrangler.jsonc` describes the Cloudflare Worker `whysosuresh-com`: its files, its code, its one route (`whysosuresh.com`) and its bindings to the database and the pages bucket.
- `test/site.test.ts` checks the site: the files served, valid HTML, links, outside resources, the headers, unused CSS, and that no file names the owner's email address; `test/people-words.test.ts` checks what the people pages say. `test/inbox-cli.test.ts` drives the command line with a played fetch. `test/talk.test.ts`, `test/threads.test.ts`, `test/threads-door.test.ts`, `test/members.test.ts`, `test/public.test.ts`, `test/people.test.ts`, `test/evaluate.test.ts`, `test/pages.test.ts`, `test/routing.test.ts` and `test/migrations.test.ts` run the Worker's bundle in Cloudflare's runtime, through Miniflare, in front of `site/` with the routing `wrangler.jsonc` declares, and with a local database; the private doors' tests play Access, signing the owner's, the sync's and invited people's tokens and its sign-in question with the harness's own keys.
- `.github/workflows/site.yaml` runs the checks on every pull request and on `main`. On `main` it also applies new migrations and deploys, with a key that can deploy Workers and edit the database, and nothing in DNS.
- `infrastructure/` declares the rest: the zone, its records, the `www` redirect, the zone's settings, the database, the pages bucket, the bot check and Access (its sign-in, its three callers, and the application on `/threads/api`) with the seat expiry, applied by Planton.

## Working on it

With the Node version in `.nvmrc`:

```sh
npm ci
npm ci --prefix deploy
npm run check
```

To run the site and its Worker locally, with a local database and Cloudflare's always-pass test keys for the bot check:

```sh
deploy/node_modules/.bin/wrangler d1 migrations apply whysosuresh-com --local --config wrangler.jsonc
npm run dev
```

Then press "Send a note" on `http://localhost:8787/`, or open `#talk/stigmer` for one topic. Locally, `/threads/` loads but every call it makes is refused: nothing signs for Access there, and the Worker admits no request Access didn't sign.

To use the threads page signed in, on made-up threads, with Access played by the test harness:

```sh
npm run dev:signed-in
```

Then open `http://localhost:8790/threads/`. `npm run dev:signed-in -- --signed-out` shows what a visitor sees, and `npm run dev:signed-in -- --member meera@example.com` what a person invited to one thread sees.

`AGENTS.md` lists the rules for changes.
